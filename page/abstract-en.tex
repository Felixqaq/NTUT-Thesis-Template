    \begin{EnAbstractDescription}
        This study presents a chest CT-based auxiliary analysis pipeline for COPD, integrating lung segmentation, quantitative feature extraction, and an interpretable classification model. Using 54 clinical CT scans, we extracted 12 clinically meaningful quantitative biomarkers, including lobar and global emphysema percentages, vascular and airway-related indices, total lung volume, and pulmonary artery diameter. A fully connected neural network was trained with stratified five-fold cross-validation to perform Normal/Abnormal classification. The model achieved 92.59\% overall accuracy and an AUC of 0.9784. Feature-level analysis further showed that emphysema-related measures and total lung volume provided the strongest discrimination. These results indicate that quantitative chest CT biomarkers can serve as effective and clinically interpretable evidence for COPD auxiliary diagnosis.
    \end{EnAbstractDescription}
